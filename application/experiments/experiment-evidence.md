### Experimental Evidence



This document presents selected visual evidence from the risk-adaptive continuous authentication research environment.



The screenshots were captured during executions of the implemented application and demonstrate selected behaviors of the continuous risk engine and its interaction with authenticated sessions.



The screenshots complement the complete experimental record documented in `experiment-results.md`. They represent selected executions and do not constitute the complete set of 27 experiments performed.



\---

#### 4\. HIGH Risk → Step-Up Authentication



These screenshots demonstrate the transition of an authenticated session into the HIGH risk band and the resulting requirement for additional authentication.



\### Risk Engine Evaluation



!\[HIGH risk step-up risk evaluation](screenshots/04-high-risk-step-up.png)



The risk engine evaluates `DOCUMENT\_DOWNLOAD` and `RESTRICTED\_DOCUMENT` signals. The resulting risk score reaches 65, which places the session in the HIGH risk band.



The engine returns a `STEP\_UP` decision and indicates that additional authentication is required before the protected operation can proceed.



**Evidence demonstrated**:

* Risk score entering the HIGH band
* HIGH risk classification
* `STEP\_UP` decision
* Step-up requirement being generated by the risk engine
* Protected operation being prevented from proceeding normally



\### Step-Up Authentication Interface



!\[Step-up authentication required](screenshots/04-high-risk-step-up-interface.png)



The application subsequently presents an additional authentication interface to the user.



The interface explicitly informs the user that the current session risk level has reached HIGH and that authentication must be performed again to continue. The displayed risk score at this stage is 64 and the risk level remains HIGH.



**Evidence demonstrated**:

* User-facing response to HIGH session risk
* Additional authentication requirement
* Communication of the current risk state to the user
* Interruption of the protected operation pending verification

!\[Risk decay](screenshots/07-risk-decay.png)



The screenshot shows `QUALIFIES NORMAL` as `YES` and demonstrates the risk state being reduced through the normal activity decay mechanism.

\---



#### 8\. No Decay Without Normal Activity



This screenshot demonstrates an important condition of the risk decay mechanism: the passage of time alone does not reduce session risk.



The session continues to generate risk-producing activity while `QUALIFIES NORMAL` remains `NO`.



!\[No decay without normal activity](screenshots/08-no-decay-without-normal-activity.png)



The screenshot shows `UNASSIGNED\_PROJECT\_ACCESS` increasing the risk score from 11 to 12. A subsequent `SENSITIVE\_RESOURCE` signal further increases the score from 12 to 14.



Because the activity does not qualify as normal, normal activity decay is not applied.



**Evidence demonstrated**:

* Explicit evaluation of normal-activity qualification
* Non-qualifying activity remaining subject to risk scoring
* Continued risk accumulation
* No normal decay while qualifying activity is absent
* Time alone being insufficient to reduce the session risk score

\---



#### Summary



The selected screenshots provide visual evidence of the principal behaviors implemented in the research environment:



1\. Authentication establishes a research session.

2\. Risk-producing signals contribute to the session risk score.

3\. Correlated activities can generate additional risk.

4\. HIGH risk triggers step-up authentication.

5\. The application presents an additional authentication requirement when session risk reaches HIGH.

6\. A subsequent evaluation can return the session to an allowed state following the step-up process.

7\. The detailed risk state exposes current and historical session risk information.

8\. Qualifying normal activity produces risk decay.

9\. Non-qualifying activity does not produce normal risk decay.

10\. New authentication establishes a new research session.

11\. Logout terminates the application session and destroys its associated risk state.
